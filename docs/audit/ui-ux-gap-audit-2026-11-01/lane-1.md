## The five findings that matter most in this lane (most serious first)
1. **Buying cannot do its job on the real store computer:** the page is told nothing about what the shop stocks, so every invoice line is "an item we do not carry", and raising a one-line order takes seven or more steps with supplier and item typed as codes nobody knows by heart.
2. **Office forms ask for codes, not things:** Product id, Supplier code, Store code, Requisition number, HSN code, Scheme code — on 11 of the 29 pages a person must look up an id elsewhere before the screen will help them (no search, no picker).
3. **Tamil is not complete where the store computer speaks:** the Today page's own notes and four page headings (Approvals, Category rules, Products to publish, Data quality) stay English after the toggle, so a Tamil-first manager reads a half-translated screen.
4. **The Offer form scrolls sideways on a phone** and shows two primary buttons at once; the pricing and supplier-bills pages have captions a person must read at 13px, and the categories page has twelve controls below the 44px bar.
5. **The accountant's "daily work" card on Supplier bills is work they may not do**, with dashes where a figure or "Not known" should be — the page opens on the wrong job for that role.

## Product-wide patterns seen (one line each)
- Store-computer notes injected into pages (`edge/store-edge/src/screen-data.ts`) are English only — every "why" beside a figure is untranslated in Tamil.
- Page headings put in the HTML `h1#who` are never repainted on the language toggle on pages built from the older template (Approvals, Category rules, Products to publish, Data quality — likely more in other lanes).
- Forms take typed ids/codes instead of a search or picker; none in this lane says "(required)" in words; none uses `sreOffice.field` (§4.1 shipped on 1 Nov, nothing in this lane has moved to it).
- Native `type="date"` inputs show the browser's locale (mm/dd/yyyy here), not the shop's dd/mm; Standing orders asks for "YYYY-MM-DD" typed as text.
- Record states are mostly plain words or a two-tone pill, not the tone chip with its icon (only Approvals and Goods receipt use `chip`).
- Refusal wording is "Head office refused:" + the server's English sentence — the reason is never Tamil and does not say whether anything was saved (the lost-link sentences do).
- The store pack does not feed the catalogue/buying pages costs, barcodes in use, shelf positions or the stock list, so margin, clash, shelf-order and invoice-line checks are honest strips rather than working features on the box.
- Machine-format dates and times on screen ("2026-11-01T16:22:56.319Z", "Trading day 2026-11-01", "As of: 2026-11-01 21:55").
- Subpage tiles truncate the page's lead to a fragment ("1.", "Saved on this scr…") — the purpose line a person reads is cut.
