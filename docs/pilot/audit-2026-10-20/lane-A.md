## 2. Top 5 blockers

1. **Nobody has decided how the pilot runs, and that decides almost everything else.**
   - The options are: the old ERP keeps billing and the new system copies it; the new till really sells one department; or wait.
   - If the new till really sells, two things have no answer yet:
     - a customer with rice (pilot) and soap (not pilot);
     - how those sales get into the shop's GST return, which today the old ERP files and which does not see the new till.
   - This needs you and your CA (A1, A2, A7).
2. **The new till's bill is not a legal GST bill.**
   - Missing: the words "Tax Invoice"; the GSTIN, unless someone types it into the header; the HSN codes; CGST and SGST shown separately.
   - Also missing: a "Bill of Supply" title for tax-free items.
   - A check for exactly these fields exists in the system, but the till's bill never uses it (A3, A4, A18).
3. **The customer cannot be handed a paper bill at all.**
   - The bill appears only on the screen.
   - No printer connection exists, not even printing through Windows.
   - Tamil words would print as "???" (A5, A6).
4. **Card and UPI are recorded only on the cashier's say-so.**
   - The cashier taps "approved" after the shop's own card machine answers. Nothing from the machine's slip is saved, so the bank's settlement cannot be matched bill by bill.
   - A refund to a card waits forever, with no instruction to the cashier (A9, A11).
5. **The store PC set-up stops before the till.**
   - Your Windows guide ends at the rollback drill.
   - It does not yet cover: installing the till, issuing each person's PIN, connecting the till to the local system, or loading the shop's name, GSTIN and bill-number prefix.
   - The people and the department are not yet named (OB-243) (A14, A15, A18, A36, A38).

A sixth item is small to fix but serious. If the till loses the reply after the store computer saved a sale, it tells the cashier "do not take money" even though the sale is saved. That could leave a phantom sale and a second sale for the same customer (A8). It is a one-day fix.

## 3. Questions only the owner can answer

**Q1. How does the pilot run? (Decision P; A1)**
- **A. Mirror:**
  - The old ERP bills every customer exactly as today.
  - A trained person also rings the pilot department's lines on the new till.
  - The new bill is not given to the customer.
  - Consequence: zero legal risk from the new bill, no printer needed yet, and GST stays with the old ERP. The evening comparison works as built. It costs staff time and proves less about the counter.
- **B. Real trading at one dedicated pilot counter:**
  - That counter sells only pilot-department baskets on the new till; the old tills sell everything else.
  - Consequence: A3, A4, A5, A7 and A9 must be fixed before day one. It is a real test of the till.
- **C. Wait** until the legal bill, the printer and the card-machine link are built and the staff have practised on the demo.
  - Consequence: safest; the pilot date moves by weeks.

**Q2. A customer with pilot and non-pilot goods (A2). Only matters if Q1 is B.**
- **A. Pilot-only counter:** a sign says "pilot items only". A mixed basket goes to an old till for everything, and those pilot items are rung on the old ERP that time. Simple for the customer. Some pilot sales then sit in the old ERP, and the evening reconciliation must count them.
- **B. Two bills at one counter:** the pilot lines on the new till, the rest on the old ERP, and two payments. Every item is billed once, but it is slower, and the customer gets two bills and pays twice.
- **C. Old ERP bills mixed baskets in full:** only all-pilot baskets use the new till. No double billing, but fewer real tests.

**Q3. Which bill is the legal one, and who files GST for pilot sales? Decide with your CA (A7).**
- **A.** The old ERP stays the legal record for everything (mirror). Nothing changes in filing.
- **B.** The new till's bills are legal for the pilot counter. Each month the CA adds the new system's GSTR-1 figures and its bill series to the old ERP's return, by hand, from a report we build. A small build; the CA's time each month.
- **C.** Pilot only in a month the CA agrees to file from both systems, with a written method first.

**Q4. Card and UPI at the new till (A9, A11).**
- **A. Cash only on the new till for the pilot:** card and UPI customers go to an old till. No reconciliation risk, but fewer customers can use it.
- **B. The shop's own card machine and UPI QR:** the cashier also types the machine's approval number. Refunds to card are done on the machine and recorded with a second person. Small build; daily matching becomes possible.
- **C. Wait for the payment provider (EX-03)** and connect the machine properly. Cleanest, but takes weeks to months and needs a contract.

**Q5. Which department, and does it have loose or weighed goods? (A20, A38)**
- **A.** Packaged/ambient groceries, barcoded only, as the 24 Sep plan says. No scale is needed.
- **B.** A department with loose goods. That needs a stamped label-printing scale and its entry in the system first.
- **C.** Name it later. The pilot cannot start until you do (OB-243).

**Q6. Goods arriving for the pilot department (A31).**
- **A.** Received in the old ERP as today (purchase order and supplier bill stay there), and the same delivery is counted into the new system the same day by a named person.
- **B.** Received in the new system only, for the pilot department. The old ERP's stock for that department is then wrong for the whole pilot.
- **C.** Pilot department deliveries booked only on fixed days, when the named person is present.

**Q7. Names for the daily reconciler and the incident owner (A36; OB-243, G10).**
- **A.** You do both for the first two weeks. Quick to start, but it uses your own time every evening.
- **B.** Name a trusted staff member as reconciler, with you as incident owner. Training is needed first.
- **C.** Hire temporary help for the parallel run. It costs money, and they need a login and training.

**Q8. If the store PC is down or the power goes (A28, A29).**
- **A.** Buy an online UPS for the PC and the network box, and write a one-page "PC down → use the old till" sheet. Small cost, and the safest of the three.
- **B.** No UPS. A power cut means the old tills only, until the PC is back. No cost, but more disruption.
- **C.** Buy a second PC as a spare later (roadmap §33 asks for a spare). Higher cost; plan it after the pilot.

**In plain English for the owner:**
- The till's core is strong, and the tests prove it today. It signs people in with their own PIN, never loses or repeats a saved sale, keeps working with no internet, and makes the cashier and the manager do the right thing for refunds, age checks and cash-up.
- What is not ready is the part the customer touches: a proper GST bill on paper, and how card payments match the bank.
- Two decisions are only yours, with your CA: how the pilot runs, and what happens with a basket from two departments.

**What to check, and how:**
1. On the demo till, ring a sale and look at the bill on the screen. You will see no "Tax Invoice", no GSTIN and no HSN.
2. Ask your CA: "If one counter bills one department from a new system, how do we file GST for it?"
3. Look at your card machine's end-of-day slip. That slip is what we would need to match against.
