## 2. Top 5 blockers, in plain English

1. **A broken hard disk would lose real sales for good (B2, B3, B4; P0).** Everything (the database, the till's unsent sales, the staff PINs, the keys) sits on one disk in one PC. A backup happens only when you remember to plug in your drive and type the command, and even then the till's own sales folder is not included. If the disk dies at 4 pm, every sale since your last backup is gone, and the system cannot even tell you which bills are missing. The roadmap says a committed sale must never be lost.

2. **Nobody has decided which bill is the legal GST bill while both systems run (L1–L4; P0 until decided).** If a sale is rung on both systems, there are two invoices for one sale. If it is rung only on the new one, the old ERP's GST returns and Tally miss it. None of the 22 CA questions covers this. At your turnover band (₹5–10 crore), a bill to a GST-registered business may also need an e-invoice number, which the new till cannot make yet.

3. **Anyone at the store PC can act as anyone (S1, S3, S4, S5, MG6; P1).** With the PC's settings file, a person can sign in as you, the CA or the reconciler. Any person in that Windows session can open the database with no password. The settings file is not locked to your account, the disk is not encrypted, and there is no step for separate Windows accounts. Every "signed by a named person" control is only as strong as who sits at that keyboard.

4. **Nobody is told when something goes wrong (M1, M4, B12, G10; P1).** Several kinds of trouble raise no alert on the store PC: a failed sync, a stuck item, a missed backup, a disk filling up, or the PC sitting at a restart screen. They would go only to an inbox nobody can open on that PC, and only if alert rules had been set, which no step does. No incident owner is named yet.

5. **Several required steps have no path you can follow on Windows (MG2, MG10b, B5, B11; P1).** Sealing the exported files (needed before the first load), writing the parallel-run plan (who reconciles, for how many days), restoring onto a new PC after a failure, and installing the next version without losing data each need hand-written programmer commands, or exist only on Linux. Customer mobile numbers also must not be taken until you name the grievance officer (S8).

## 3. Questions only the owner can answer

**Q1. How are backups taken during the pilot (B2–B4)?**
- **A.** Buy a second USB drive that stays plugged in. The system backs up by itself every hour while the shop is open (database and till data). You swap your own drive in once a day. About ₹5,000 for the drive, plus a medium build. A dead disk then loses at most about one hour.
- **B.** A second computer keeps a live copy all the time. Nearly nothing is lost, but it means buying a PC and a large build.
- **C.** A named person runs the backup by hand at day close. You accept in writing that a dead disk can lose up to one day of pilot-department sales, re-entered from paper or the old ERP.

**Q2. Off-site copy (B8, B9). Your OB-240 says "data never leaves the shop", and the roadmap asks for an off-site copy.**
- **A.** A second drive, encrypted, that a named custodian takes home each week. Only unreadable files leave the shop.
- **B.** Both drives stay in a locked cupboard in the shop. You accept in writing that a fire or theft could lose everything, with a target release to fix it.
- **C.** An encrypted copy to a cloud folder. This reverses OB-240.

**Q3. Sign-in on the store PC (S1).**
- **A.** Turn on the shop's own sign-in server on that PC, so each person has their own password and second check. This is medium-large work and uses more memory, which matters with 8 GB.
- **B.** Keep the stand-in sign-in for the one-department pilot. You alone hold the PC's admin account, the disk is encrypted, and you initial each signature on paper. You accept the stand-in in writing until the release you name.
- **C.** No real data until a production sign-in is chosen. This delays the pilot.

**Q4. Billing while both systems run (L1–L3). Please also ask the CA to confirm in writing.**
- **A.** Pilot-department sales are billed only on the new system. The CA adds the new system's monthly GST figures and daily day-book into the return and Tally. The old ERP holds no pilot-department sales, so the daily comparison covers stock and receipts only.
- **B.** Every sale is rung on both systems. Only the old ERP's bill is the tax invoice, and the new system's paper is marked "not a tax invoice". This needs a change, and it doubles the cashiers' work.
- **C.** The new system's bill is the tax invoice. The old ERP gets a daily summary entry that is not an invoice. This needs the CA's method.

**Q5. Customer data in the pilot (S8).**
- **A.** Guest sales only. No mobile numbers or loyalty points until the grievance officer is named. This is the simplest and is a small instruction or switch.
- **B.** Name the grievance officer now, and publish the English and Tamil notice before the first real customer.
- **C.** Take mobile numbers anyway. Not recommended: there is no notice and no contact person.

**Q6. Customers who need a GST bill (B2B) during the pilot (L4).**
- **A.** Bill them on the old ERP only. The new till is for ordinary shoppers.
- **B.** Turn on live e-invoicing in the new system. This needs a GST suvidha provider and a large piece of work. Please also confirm with the CA whether turnover has ever passed ₹5 crore.

**Q7. Where does the pilot till run (S3; also lane A)?**
- **A.** On a separate till PC, with the store PC used only by you. This needs a small change so the till can reach the store PC.
- **B.** On the store PC itself. Cashiers then get a separate, locked Windows account that can open only the till.

**Q8. Keys after a disk failure (B6).**
- **A.** An encrypted copy of the settings file goes on your backup drive, opened with a passphrase only you keep.
- **B.** No copy. After a failure, new keys are made and every till is set up again, adding about 1–2 hours to recovery.

**Q9. What kind of business is SRE Hyper Market (L5)?** The legal backup rules differ by type.
- **A.** Private or public limited company. The Companies Act daily backup rule also applies.
- **B.** Partnership or LLP.
- **C.** Sole proprietor.

**Q10. The label on the real copy (H7).**
- **A.** Keep "rehearsal" until the cutover GO, and accept in writing that the migration tools stay switched on there.
- **B.** Add a "pilot" label that allows the one-department load and the parallel run but refuses reversals and bulk loads. Small to medium work.
