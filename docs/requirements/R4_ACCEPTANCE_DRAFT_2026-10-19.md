| B20 — Plastic EPR register for the shop's own-brand packaging | Every product you mark as the shop's own brand carries its plastic packaging category and its weight per unit. One without them is listed as an exception ("packaging declaration missing") and is never counted as zero. For a financial year, the system totals the plastic put on the market per category, to the gram, from the units received and produced, as a sortable file for the CPCB portal. Nothing is filed automatically. The EPR registration number and any exemption are settings with their reference; until the CA or EPR consultant confirms them they show "not confirmed" (OB-212). (Source: gap analysis B20; Plastic Waste Management Rules 2016, EPR.) | |
| B21 — E-waste handover and take-back register | An e-waste handover (old tills, scales, lights) is recorded with the weight and the recycler's registration number. A handover with no registration number is refused ("recycler not registered"). An item a customer brings back for disposal is recorded as a take-back, never as a sale return, and is kept out of saleable stock. A disposed fixed asset with no e-waste handover is listed as an exception. Whether the shop must also register as a brand owner is the CA's or consultant's answer (OB-212); until then it shows "not confirmed". (Source: gap analysis B21; E-Waste (Management) Rules 2022.) | |
| B22 — Battery take-back register | A used battery a customer returns is recorded as a take-back with its type and weight. It sits in a take-back location that can never be sold from ("not saleable: take-back stock"). It leaves only on a handover to a recycler with a recorded registration number; any other handover is refused ("recycler not registered"). Take-back stock on hand equals what was taken back less what was handed over; a difference is an exception. Battery brand-owner duties show "not confirmed" until the CA or consultant answers (OB-212). (Source: gap analysis B22; Battery Waste Management Rules 2022.) | |
| B23 — Online store display duties and grievance officer | Every product in the online store shows, before payment: the MRP; the selling price with tax included; the delivery fee and the total; the best-before or expiry date where the product carries one; the country of origin; the shop's legal name, address and GSTIN; the return and refund terms; and the grievance officer's name and contact. A product missing any of these cannot be published online ("online listing incomplete: <field>"); it still sells in the shop. An online complaint is acknowledged within 48 hours, and one not resolved within one month reaches the owner as an exception. The privacy and terms pages open from every page of the store without signing in. **This item cannot pass until you name the grievance officer and give the contact (OB-223, pending).** (Source: gap analysis B23; Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules 2020, Rule 5.) | |
| A26 — GST TCS (section 52): not applicable | The online store sells only the shop's own goods (OB-211). No TCS is collected, and the GST page shows "TCS (section 52): not applicable: own goods only". Listing another seller's item online, including a concession tenant's, is refused ("other seller's goods: not sold online"). The CA is asked to confirm "not applicable" in writing. (Source: gap analysis A26; CGST Act 2017 s.52.) | |
| D-28 — Digital receipts, only for members who ask | A member gets digital receipts only after switching them on in their own app (OB-214); no one is switched on by default. When a banked sale names such a member, a copy appears under "My receipts" in their app after the till syncs, with the same bill number, lines, tax and payments as the printed bill; a card shows only its last four digits. A member who has not switched it on gets no copy. Another customer's receipt is refused ("not your receipt"). The till still prints the paper bill with the network off, and a digital receipt never delays or changes a sale. Sending by SMS or WhatsApp is built switched off, and nothing is sent while switched off; the app says so (OB-222). (Source: gap analysis D-28; roadmap D08-FR-05.) | |
| C15 — Statutory digital payslip | Every payslip carries each particular on the CA's list. One missing a listed particular cannot be issued ("payslip particular missing: <name>"). A payslip not issued by the pay date reaches the owner as an exception. An employee sees only their own payslip. Until the CA fills the list, each particular shows "not confirmed" on the payslip and nothing is guessed (OB-208). Approving, locking and the bank file stay on hold; no real payslip is issued and no pay is released (OB-209). (Source: gap analysis C15; addendum §1.3; Code on Wages 2019 s.50.) | |
| C16 — Hours worked, overtime and minimum wage | Each employee's hours actually worked per day come from attendance and are shown on the payslip. Hours above the normal daily or weekly hours are overtime, paid at no less than twice the ordinary hourly rate; a lower rate is refused ("overtime below 2×"). Once the CA fills the minimum wage for an employee's class, a month's pay below it is refused ("below minimum wage"). Until the CA fills them, the minimum wage and the normal hours show "not confirmed", and the pay run says "minimum wage not checked" or "overtime limit not set"; it never assumes a figure (OB-208). Approving, locking and the bank file stay on hold (OB-209). (Source: gap analysis C16; addendum §1.3; Code on Wages 2019.) | |
| C19 — EPF and ESI contributions | Every EPF and ESI rate, wage ceiling and headcount threshold is a setting that shows "not confirmed" until the CA fills it (OB-208); the figures in the gap analysis are not used as settled. Once confirmed, PF and ESI are worked out from those settings only after the shop's headcount reaches each threshold. Below a threshold, that contribution is not deducted and the payslip says why. The month's contribution totals equal the sum of the payslip lines to the paisa; a difference is an exception. Approving, locking and the bank file stay on hold (OB-209). (Source: gap analysis C19; addendum §1.3.) | |
| D-10 — Supplier scorecards | Each supplier's scorecard is built from its own purchase orders, goods receipts and matched invoices, with no typed figures. It shows fill rate, on time in full (the share of order lines delivered complete by the agreed date), lead-time reliability, invoice accuracy (invoiced against agreed price) and rejection rate. A supplier with no delivery in the period reads "not rated", never a score. A corrected receipt replaces its figure and is never counted twice. (Source: gap analysis D-10; addendum §3.) | |
| D-13 — Planogram check with a photo | A shelf check may carry a photo. The score per bay compares the counted facings with the approved planogram and names every gap ("empty, stock in back", "empty, none in building", "never counted", "counted too long ago"). Image recognition is built as a switched-off link with a test stand-in, and nothing is sent while switched off: no photo leaves the shop, and the score says "counted by staff" (OB-219). (Source: gap analysis D-13; addendum §3.) | |
| D-18 — Competitor prices draft a price change | When a competitor's latest recorded price for the same product is 5% or more below the shop's, and that sighting is under 14 days old, the system drafts a price change (OB-216; both figures are settings you can change). A sighting 14 days old or more is not used ("sighting too old"). The draft is never above MRP and never below cost or the margin floor ("draft refused: below margin floor"). Nothing changes a price until a person approves it through the existing price approval; no AI makes or commits the change. (Source: gap analysis D-18; addendum §3.) | |
| D-21 — Loss-prevention case joining till, shrink and counts | For a product or a cashier and a period, one view puts together the till exceptions (voids, no-sale opens, price overrides, refunds), the recorded waste and shrink, and the count differences. It proposes a case; a person decides whether to open it. Nothing is opened, or blamed on anyone, by itself. A CCTV clip is recorded only as a reference (camera, time from and to, who collected it); no video is stored or analysed (OB-217). A case cannot close without an outcome, and "unfounded" is a valid one. (Source: gap analysis D-21; addendum §3.) | |
| D-22 — Personal offers from purchase history | From a consenting adult member's own purchase history, the system drafts personal offers. A person approves each batch before any is issued. A member without both profiling and marketing consent gets none ("no consent"); a member under 18 or with no age confirmed gets none ("child or age unknown"). No member gets more than your cap of 2 offers in 7 days (OB-42). An offer never takes a price below cost or above MRP. No AI issues or commits an offer. (Source: gap analysis D-22; addendum §3.) | |
| D-23 — Bought-together analysis | From banked sales for a period and department, the system lists product pairs bought together more often than chance, each with its basket count, support, confidence and lift. Pairs seen in fewer than the minimum number of baskets are not shown. Returns are taken out of the baskets they came from. Suggestions (place side by side, bundle, promotion) are drafts; a promotion made from one goes through the existing promotion approval. (Source: gap analysis D-23; addendum §3.) | |
| D-24 — Festivals and local events in the forecast | A manager records a festival or local event for the store (dates and expected effect). The next forecasts and order proposals use it with no extra step, and each figure it changed names the event. The 7-day forecast is corrected from the most recent days' actual sales, with the back-test error shown beside it. An effect never back-tested shows as "manager's estimate". The weather feed is built as a switched-off link, and nothing is sent or fetched while switched off (OB-220). (Source: gap analysis D-24; addendum §3.) | |
| WF-16 — Online return after delivery | For a delivered online order, the customer raises a return in the app within the window set for that product's category (OB-213). The shop schedules a collection and receives the goods back with a count. A person chooses the disposition: back to sellable, quarantine, or waste with a reason. The refund goes back to the original payment only for lines received, and the customer sees each step in the app. Refused by name: a return outside its category's window ("return window closed"); an item on your non-returnable list ("not returnable"); a second refund for the same line ("already refunded"). A refund the payment provider has not confirmed shows as "waiting", never as done. **The window per category and the non-returnable list come from you; this item cannot pass until you give them.** (Source: roadmap WF-16; OB-58.) | |

A coupon still cannot be redeemed twice or after expiry. (Source: roadmap M17-FR-02, D07-FR-04; OB-58.) | |

The app works the same without an image. (Source: roadmap D01-FR-06; OB-60, OB-74.) | |
